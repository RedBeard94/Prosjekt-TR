Trend arrows and automation.

What the arrows on the sensor mean, and how the pump can adjust the insulin by itself. The drawings are simplified, so your own screen may look different.

Made for Dexcom sensor and Tandem pump. If you have another pump, this may be misleading for you.

Remember: This is a tool, not a final answer. Check important numbers with your diabetes team.

The sensor screen

  • 1
    The numberWhat the sensor shows right now.
  • 2
    The arrowWhich way the number is going, and how fast.
  • 3
    The curveThe last hours. It shows whether the number has been going up or down for a while.

What the arrows mean on MiniMed 780G

Up to three arrows, up or down. The arrows show how much the sensor value has changed in the last 20 minutes.

  • Three arrows upRising fastest. More than 0.17 mmol/L per minute (more than 3.3 mmol/L in the last 20 minutes).
  • Two arrows upRising fast. 0.11 to 0.17 mmol/L per minute (2.22 to 3.33 in the last 20 minutes).
  • One arrow upRising. 0.06 to 0.11 mmol/L per minute (1.11 to 2.22 in the last 20 minutes).
  • No arrowChanging slowly. Less than 0.06 mmol/L per minute.
  • One arrow downFalling. 0.06 to 0.11 mmol/L per minute.
  • Two arrows downFalling fast. 0.11 to 0.17 mmol/L per minute.
  • Three arrows downFalling fastest. More than 0.17 mmol/L per minute.
Source: Medtronic's Norwegian user guide for MiniMed 780G. What to do at each arrow is decided by your diabetes team.
Guardian 4: The same limits are in Medtronic's English system guide for MiniMed 780G with Guardian 4 (given there in mg/dL).
Instinct: The arrows are shown by the pump. We have not read a guide that mentions Instinct specifically. Ask your diabetes team if anything looks different.

What the arrows mean on FreeStyle Libre 3 Plus

Five steps, from fastest up to fastest down. Libre has no double arrow.

  • Arrow upRising fast. More than 0.1 mmol/L per minute.
  • Arrow up-rightRising. 0.06 to 0.1 mmol/L per minute.
  • Arrow straightChanging slowly. Less than 0.06 mmol/L per minute.
  • Arrow down-rightFalling. 0.06 to 0.1 mmol/L per minute.
  • Arrow downFalling fast. More than 0.1 mmol/L per minute.
Source: Abbott's Norwegian user manuals for FreeStyle Libre 3 (reader and app). What to do at each arrow is decided by your diabetes team. If you are unsure about a value, contact a healthcare professional before you act. Pump automation (such as Control-IQ) is not explained here for Libre yet.
With Omnipod 5 you start and connect the sensor in the Omnipod 5 app, not in the Libre app. The app shows the sensor value together with the arrow. Source: Omnipod 5 user guide (Norwegian).
With Tandem t:slim X2 you start the Libre 3 Plus sensor in the Tandem t:slim app. The sensor can only be connected to one device at a time, so it must not be connected to the Libre app or reader first. Start-up takes 1 hour with no sensor values. The pump does not use the arrow when it calculates a bolus. Source: Tandem's Norwegian guide.

What the arrows mean on Dexcom

Seven steps, from fastest up to fastest down. A double arrow means faster than a single arrow.

  • Two arrows upRising fastest.
  • One arrow upRising quickly.
  • Arrow diagonally upRising at a steady pace.
  • Arrow straight aheadThe number stays about the same.
  • Arrow diagonally downFalling at a steady pace.
  • One arrow downFalling quickly.
  • Two arrows downFalling fastest.
How many mmol/L per minute each arrow stands for, and what to do for each arrow, is in the user guide and with the diabetes team.

Control-IQ

The pump looks at what the sensor thinks the value will be in 30 minutes, and chooses based on that.

10.0mmol/L8.96.253.9
DeliversGives an automatic correction bolus if the value is expected to go above 10.0.
IncreasesIncreases the basal dose if the value is expected to go above 8.9.
KeepsUses the settings in your active profile.
ReducesReduces the basal dose if the value is expected to go below 6.25.
StopsStops the basal dose if the value is expected to go below 3.9.

With no activity on, the pump tries to keep the value between 6.25 and 8.9 mmol/L. The pump goes by what the value is expected to be, not just what it is now.

Basal-IQ

The simpler function. It only watches for low values.

  1. 1
    Looks aheadThe pump predicts the values 30 minutes ahead.
  2. 2
    Stops the insulinIf the value is expected to go below 4.4, or a sensor reading is below 3.9.
  3. 3
    Starts againThe insulin comes back automatically when the values begin to rise.
Basal-IQ applies to t:slim X2 with Dexcom G6. Control-IQ applies to both G6 and G7.

Control-IQ+ in Tandem t:slim X2

The numbers here come from Tandem's Norwegian guide for Control-IQ+ (version 7.10). The pump looks 30 minutes ahead.

  1. 1
    Automatic correction bolusWhen the value is expected to be 10 mmol/L or more, and the pump is already increasing or giving maximum insulin, it can give a correction bolus. The target is 6.1 mmol/L. Maximum 6 units, and at most once every 60 minutes. The pump makes no sound or vibration when it starts.
  2. 2
    Sleep and exerciseYou set up a sleep schedule or switch sleep or exercise on yourself. During sleep the target range is 6.25 to 6.7, and no automatic correction bolus is given. During exercise it is 7.8 to 8.9, and insulin is stopped when the value is expected to go below 4.4.
  3. 3
    Without sensor valuesAfter 20 minutes without a sensor value, the pump goes back to the basal in your personal profile.
The insulin duration is fixed at 5 hours when Control-IQ+ is on. The limits depend on the pump software. For Control-IQ, Tandem and Rubin write on their websites that it should not be used under 6 years, under 10 units of insulin a day or under 25 kg. For Control-IQ+, the technical guide says it should not be used under 2 years, under 5 units a day or under 9 kg. Ask your diabetes team which one you have. What suits you is decided by your diabetes team. Sources: Tandem, website (Norwegian) and Tandem t:slim X2 with Control-IQ+, technical user guide (Norwegian).

mylife YpsoPump: you control the pump yourself

YpsoPump is not connected to the sensor and has no bolus calculator. You read the sensor in the sensor's own app or reader.

  1. 1
    Read the sensorLook at the number and arrow in the sensor's app or reader. The arrows are explained above for your sensor.
  2. 2
    Decide the bolus from your planThe pump does not calculate a bolus. It gives the amount you set. Your diabetes team has taught you how to find the amount.
  3. 3
    Choose the type of bolusThe pump has standard bolus, extended bolus (15 minutes to 12 hours), combination bolus and blind bolus. You can also set a temporary basal rate from 0 to 90% or 110 to 200% for 15 minutes to 24 hours.
The pump's user guide says blood sugar should be checked at least 4 times a day, ideally 6 to 8. Some people use YpsoPump with the CamAPS FX app, which controls the pump automatically. It is not explained here yet. Follow the plan from your diabetes team. Source: mylife YpsoPump, user guide.

Omnipod DASH: you enter the value yourself

DASH has no automation and is not connected to the sensor. You read the sensor in the sensor's own app or reader.

  1. 1
    Read the sensorLook at the number and arrow in the sensor's app or reader. The arrows are explained above for your sensor.
  2. 2
    Enter the value in the PDMYou can enter a reading from a CGM that is indicated for insulin dosing. The bolus calculator uses the value you enter.
  3. 3
    Does it match how you feel?If you have symptoms that do not match the number, contact your health professional.
The PDM suggests no bolus when the value is below the minimum BG you have set, and suggests a correction only when the value is above "Correct above". You have the final say on how much is given. Messages appear for values below 3.9 and above 13.9 mmol/L. Follow the plan from your diabetes team. The pump does not adjust insulin by itself. Source: Omnipod DASH, user guide (Norwegian).

Accu-Chek Solo: you enter the value yourself

Solo has no automation and is not connected to the sensor. You read the sensor in the sensor's own app or reader.

  1. 1
    Read the sensorLook at the number and arrow in the sensor's app or reader. The arrows are explained above for your sensor.
  2. 2
    Enter the value in SoloIn Solo you choose Measure and enter the glucose value by hand. A sensor value counts for a bolus advice for only 15 minutes.
  3. 3
    Check with a fingerstickIf the number does not match how you feel, take a blood glucose test with a fingerstick.
If the value is below the low-glucose warning limit, Solo gives no bolus advice but recommends carbohydrates. The pump does not adjust insulin by itself. What you do at each arrow is decided by your diabetes team. Source: Accu-Chek Solo, user guide (Norwegian).

The Instinct sensor

Made by Abbott for MiniMed systems.

  • The pump must be upgraded first. It needs a software version that supports Instinct. Without the upgrade the sensor does not work.
  • You need a compatible smartphone, the MiniMed Mobile app and a CareLink Personal user. If you cannot use the app, tell the treatment aids service when you order.
  • Up to 15 days. Worn on the back of the upper arm.
  • Approved from age 2, also for pregnant women. Children aged 2 to 12 need a carer over 18 to help. The system you use it in may have a different age limit.
  • Water: 1 metre for 30 minutes.
  • Hard exercise and sweat can make the sensor come loose. If the number does not match how you feel, do a fingerstick and check the sensor is attached.
  • The adhesive can irritate the skin. Remove the sensor and talk to a health professional.
  • Storage: 2 to 28 °C. Do not freeze.
Warm-up time and how to start the sensor in the pump are not in the sources we have. Ask your diabetes team.

Sources: Abbott and Medtronic, package leaflet "Instinct Sensor", and Helse Fonna: Upgrading to the Instinct sensor for MiniMed 780G (Norwegian, updated 10 Sep 2026).

The Guardian 4 sensor

Used with the Guardian 4 transmitter and MiniMed 780G.

  • Up to 7 days (170 hours from when the transmitter is connected to the sensor).
  • From age 7. Worn on the back of the upper arm. Ages 7 to 17: also the upper buttocks. From age 18: also the abdomen. Avoid 5 cm around the navel.
  • Warm-up: 2 hours after you start a new sensor. Then the sensor values appear.
  • No calibration needed. The pump can still use fingerstick readings you enter to calibrate the sensor.
  • Paracetamol (acetaminophen) can make sensor values read too high.
  • Storage: 2 to 27 °C. Do not freeze.
Whether you can make treatment decisions from the sensor value without a fingerstick is stated differently in Medtronic's guides. Follow what your diabetes team has told you.

Sources: Medtronic, "Guardian 4 sensor user guide" (Norwegian) and "MiniMed 780G System User Guide with Guardian 4 sensor" (English). More guides: Medtronic, guides and manuals (Norwegian).

SmartGuard

The automatic feature in MiniMed 780G. The pump adjusts the insulin by itself based on sensor values, trends and recent insulin.

  1. 1
    Adjusts basal insulinThe pump changes the basal dose based on the sensor values, the trends and the insulin you recently received.
  2. 2
    Gives small correction bolusesIf the sensor value is high, the pump can give a small correction automatically. The menu is called Autokorreksjoner (auto corrections).
  3. 3
    TargetYou can choose a target of 5.5, 6.1 or 6.7 mmol/L. 5.5 is the default. A temporary target of 8.3 mmol/L is available for activity (the menu is called Midl. mål).
How you use SmartGuard is decided by your diabetes team. Source: Medtronic's training guide.

Stopp før lav SG (Stop before low)

The simpler feature when you use the pump in manual mode with a sensor. It only watches for low values.

  1. 1
    Watches for a risk of lowThe pump follows the sensor values.
  2. 2
    Stops basal insulinWhen the pump sees a risk of low blood sugar, it pauses the basal insulin.
  3. 3
    Stops for up to two hoursThe insulin is paused for a maximum of two hours. The feature is off by default, and is used in manual mode, not together with SmartGuard.
The limits the pump uses are in the user guide and with your diabetes team. Source: Medtronic's training guide.

SmartAdjust

The automatic feature in Omnipod 5 (Automated Mode). The Pod adjusts the insulin by itself based on the sensor values.

  1. 1
    Looks aheadSmartAdjust predicts where your glucose will be 60 minutes ahead.
  2. 2
    Adjusts every 5 minutesThe Pod uses the sensor values to make automatic insulin decisions every 5 minutes, to bring the glucose toward the target glucose (Målglukose). It can also stop the insulin.
  3. 3
    TargetThe target glucose (Målglukose) can be set from 6.1 to 8.3 mmol/L, in steps of 0.55, with up to eight time segments a day. When Activity mode is on, the target is 8.3 mmol/L.
You still have to enter meal boluses yourself. How you use SmartAdjust is decided by your diabetes team. Source: Omnipod 5 user guide (Norwegian).

When the sensor values are missing

Omnipod 5 has its own status when the Pod does not get sensor values.

  1. 1
    Automated: LimitedAfter 20 minutes without sensor values the pump switches to “Automatisert: Begrenset” (Automated: Limited).
  2. 2
    Basal insulin without adjustingIt then uses the lower of the basal rates in Manual Mode and your personal basal rate, and does not adjust up or down for the glucose.
Always switch to Manual Mode if you feel you are getting inaccurate sensor values. Source: Omnipod 5 user guide (Norwegian).
