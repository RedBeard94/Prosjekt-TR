Information for adults

The helper

Short facts about type 1 diabetes for teachers, coaches, grandparents and other adults who look after a child with diabetes. The child and the parents know the situation best.

1What is type 1 diabetes?

Type 1 diabetes is a lifelong illness. The body does not make insulin. Insulin is needed for the body to use sugar as energy.

The child gets insulin from a pump or a pen. The illness is not contagious. It is not caused by eating too much sugar.

2Low blood sugar

Blood sugar can drop quickly. The child then needs help right away.

Signs
  • Sweating
  • Shaking
  • Pale skin
  • Hunger
  • Restless or irritable
  • Tired or absent
What to do
1
Give fast carbohydrates

For example glucose tablets, juice or squash (not diet).

2
Wait 10 to 15 minutes

Keep the child calm, and keep an eye on them.

3
Give some food afterwards

For example a slice of bread or crispbread.

4
Tell the parents

The phone number is in the child's bag.

Remember! If the child is unconscious or having seizures: do not give food or drink. See Emergency.

3Pump and sensor

Many children use an insulin pump and a glucose sensor. The equipment can beep or vibrate. That is normal.

If it beeps: Ask the child what it means. If you are unsure, contact the parents.

4Food and drink

Children with diabetes can eat the same as other children. The child keeps track of what they eat.

Remember! The child must always be allowed to eat. Do not skip meals.

5Activity

During PE, swimming and trips, the child may need extra food. The parents usually tell you in advance.

Agree with the parents on what to do before the activity.
Emergency: see guidance
