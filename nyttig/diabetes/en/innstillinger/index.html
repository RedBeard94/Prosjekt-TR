On MiniMed 780G the fields above have other names: Correction factor is called Insulin sensitivity factor (Insulinsensitivitetsfaktor), Target value is called BS-mål (blood glucose target), and Insulin duration is called Aktivt insulin-tid (active insulin time). Medtronic writes that you should always use a fingerstick reading when you enter a blood glucose value in the pump. Source: Medtronic's Norwegian user guide.
On Omnipod 5 the fields above have other names: Carbohydrate ratio is called Insulin-to-carb ratio (Insulin-til-KH-Forhold), Target value is called Target glucose (Målglukose), and Insulin duration is called Duration of insulin activity (Varighet av Insulinaktivitet). When you enter blood glucose in the SmartBolus calculator, only use a reading from the last 10 minutes, or tap BRUK SENSOR (use sensor). If the sensor values do not match your symptoms, you must always check your blood glucose with a meter. Source: Omnipod 5 user guide (Norwegian).
On Accu-Chek Solo the fields above are called something else: Correction factor is called Insulinfølsomhet, Target is called Målområde (lower and upper limit), and Insulin duration is called Virketid. Solo is not connected to the sensor. You enter the sensor value yourself, and it counts for bolus advice for only 15 minutes. Source: Accu-Chek Solo, user guide (Norwegian).
On Omnipod DASH the fields above are called something else: Carb ratio is called Insulin-til-KH-forhold (IKH-forhold), Target is called BS-mål, and Insulin duration is called Varighet av insulinaktivitet. DASH is not connected to the sensor. You enter the sensor value yourself. Source: Omnipod DASH, user guide (Norwegian).
On mylife YpsoPump there are no fields for carb ratio, correction factor, target or insulin duration. The pump has bolus step, bolus limit, basal rate limit and basal program A and B. Your diabetes team decides which numbers apply to you. Source: mylife YpsoPump, user guide.
On mylife YpsoPump with CamAPS FX health professionals enter the numbers in the app. Among other things you must enter body weight (10 to 300 kg) and the average total daily insulin dose over the last 5 days (5 to 350 units) (p. 28). The bolus calculator also has a target, correction factor, insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio and duration of insulin action (2 to 8 hours) (p. 61 to 63). The pump settings must be set correctly by health professionals before use (p. 6). Record all meals and boluses in the system (p. 6). Source: CamAPS FX, user guide (Norwegian, PDF).
