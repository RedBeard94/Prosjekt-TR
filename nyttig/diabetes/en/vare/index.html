Who are they?

Oda and Meny: Norwegian online grocery shops. You can see if the food is sold and what it costs.

Matinfo: a food database where the makers enter their own numbers and ingredients.

Open Food Facts: an open database where users add products. It has many products, but the numbers can be wrong.

The links open the websites outside the app. You need internet.

How to find carbohydrates

Tap a shop to see where the number is.

Always read “Carbohydrates” per 100 g. “Sugars” are part of that number. Do not add them together.

Oda
  1. Open the product and scroll down to “Innhold”.
  2. Tap the tab Næringsinnhold (nutrition).
  3. Read Karbohydrater under “pr. 100g/ml”.
Meny
  1. Search, and use the tab Varer (products). Open the product.
  2. Scroll down and tap Næringsinnhold per 100 g/ml.
  3. Read Karbohydrater.

Meny takes a few seconds to show results. If you cannot find the product, it may not be in the store Meny has chosen.

Matinfo
  1. Pick the filter Dagligvare (groceries) on the left. Otherwise you get a lot of catering products.
  2. Open the product and scroll to Næringsinnhold.
  3. Read Karbohydrat under “Pr. 100 gram”.

The numbers come from the producer.

Open Food Facts
  1. Open the product and scroll to Ernæringsinnhold.
  2. Look in the column for 100 g / 100 ml.
  3. Read the row Karbohydrat.

The numbers are entered by users. If it says the product page is not complete, do not rely on them.

The page looks a little different from product to product. Always check against the package in your hand.

Always check the number against the package in your hand. If you are unsure, ask an adult or your diabetes team.

This page only makes search links. It does not fetch or store anything. Matinfo, Open Food Facts, Oda and Meny are separate services with their own terms. The sites are Norwegian.
