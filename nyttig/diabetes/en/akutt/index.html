Emergency. What do you do?

Follow the steps below. If in doubt, call 113.

Is the person awake and conscious?

1
Give fast carbohydrates right away
  • Glucose tablets, as many as the plan from your diabetes team says
  • A glass of juice or squash (not diet)
  • A few sugar cubes
2
Wait 10–15 minutes

See if they feel better.

3
Give slow carbohydrates

Give a slice of bread, crispbread or some other food once the blood sugar is on its way up, so it does not drop again.

Is the person unconscious or having seizures?

1
Call 113 right away!
2
Do not give food or drink by mouth

Risk of choking.

3
Place the person in the recovery position
4
Do you have glucagon?

An injection or nasal spray can be given while you wait for the ambulance, if you know how to use it.

Is the person awake and thinking clearly?

1
Check blood sugar and ketones

If you have the equipment.

2
Follow the plan from the diabetes team

It says what to do about insulin. The app does not tell you how much to take.

3
Drink water

Do not drink anything with sugar.

4
Check again after a while

If it does not get better, call the out-of-hours service 116 117.

Call 113 right away if the person:

!
is confused, dizzy or faints
!
keeps vomiting
!
has a strong smell of acetone (like nail polish remover) or heavy breathing
!
has stomach pain, is very thirsty or very tired and the blood sugar does not go down

When it is urgent

Out-of-hours clinic (legevakt): 116 117For illness that cannot wait until daytime
Emergency number: 113For serious, acute illness. Use the Call 113 button at the top.

Talk to your diabetes team about what to do when blood sugar is low or high. Write it down in My profile, under My contacts.
