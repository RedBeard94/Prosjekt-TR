Trend arrows and automation.

What the arrows on the sensor mean, and how the pump can adjust the insulin by itself. The drawings are simplified, so your own screen may look different.

The sensor screen

  • 1
    The numberWhat the sensor shows right now.
  • 2
    The arrowWhich way the number is going, and how fast.
  • 3
    The curveThe last hours. It shows whether the number has been going up or down for a while.

What the arrows mean on Dexcom

Seven steps, from fastest up to fastest down. A double arrow means faster than a single arrow.

  • Two arrows upRising fastest.
  • One arrow upRising quickly.
  • Arrow diagonally upRising at a steady pace.
  • Arrow straight aheadThe number stays about the same.
  • Arrow diagonally downFalling at a steady pace.
  • One arrow downFalling quickly.
  • Two arrows downFalling fastest.
How many mmol/L per minute each arrow stands for, and what to do for each arrow, is in the user guide and with the diabetes team.

Control-IQ

The pump looks at what the sensor thinks the value will be in 30 minutes, and chooses based on that.

10.0mmol/L8.96.253.9
DeliversGives an automatic correction bolus if the value is expected to go above 10.0.
IncreasesIncreases the basal dose if the value is expected to go above 8.9.
KeepsUses the settings in your active profile.
ReducesReduces the basal dose if the value is expected to go below 6.25.
StopsStops the basal dose if the value is expected to go below 3.9.

The target range is 3.9 to 10 mmol/L. The pump goes by what the value is expected to be, not just what it is now.

Basal-IQ

The simpler function. It only watches for low values.

  1. 1
    Looks aheadThe pump predicts the values 30 minutes ahead.
  2. 2
    Stops the insulinIf the value is expected to go below 4.4, or a sensor reading is below 3.9.
  3. 3
    Starts againThe insulin comes back automatically when the values begin to rise.
Basal-IQ applies to t:slim X2 with Dexcom G6. Control-IQ applies to both G6 and G7.

The page is an aid for understanding the screen and does not replace training and advice from the diabetes team. The pump cannot stop all high and low values, and it only works when the sensor and pump are connected. For acute illness, call the out-of-hours clinic (legevakt) on 116 117, or 113 in an emergency.
